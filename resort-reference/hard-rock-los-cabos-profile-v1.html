The resort

A big, loud, well-run party resort — and one hard limitation to name up front

Everything here is scaled for a crowd. The one thing it cannot give a couple is a swimmable ocean.

Hard Rock Hotel Los Cabos opened in 2018 as the brand’s first purpose-built all-inclusive in Mexico — 639 rooms and suites in the Diamante development at the southern tip of Baja. Every room is at least 522 sq ft with a terrace and a hydro spa tub. Six pools, a music-themed everything, a bowling alley in the Moonlight lounge, and 60,000 sq ft of event space including a ballroom that seats 1,500.

Name the beach issue in the first five minutes of the call. This property sits on the Pacific side, not the Sea of Cortez. The resort itself warns that currents are dangerous even for strong swimmers and asks guests to keep away from the shoreline. A beach ceremony on the sand is lovely and completely normal here — but nobody swims in that ocean, and a couple picturing a snorkelling week will be disappointed. The six pools carry all of the water time.

Where it wins. Group capacity and entertainment. The Muse Terrace and Harmony Ballroom can absorb weddings that most Cabo properties would have to split, the Rock Suites come with a personal assistant, and the resort’s own energy does a lot of the work for a couple who want a loud, late celebration rather than a quiet one.

Transfer runs long. Roughly 51–55 km from Los Cabos International, 45 to 75 minutes depending on traffic, because it is past Cabo San Lucas rather than on the corridor. Build that into arrival-day planning.

Accommodation

Eleven categories, nothing under 522 square feet

Every room and suite has a terrace with a hydro spa tub. Most categories work out to three adults, or two adults plus one teenager (13–17), or two adults plus two children (4–12); an infant in a crib does not count against the maximum. All Rock Suites include a personal assistant and Rock Royalty amenities.

Club level

Rock Royalty

A concierge tier rather than a lounge-and-lounge-bar club — the perks are service and spa, not a private restaurant.

  • Dedicated telephone concierge, 7 a.m.–11 p.m.
  • Complimentary 25-minute couples massage at Rock Spa®
  • Priority reservations for the Picks®, Tracks® and Wax® programmes
  • Priority reservations for Balinese beds (extra cost)
  • Upgraded full-size Rock Spa® bath products
  • Aromatherapy and pillow menus
  • Welcome petit fours and snacks
  • Loofah and sleeping mask
  • Complimentary beach bag
  • Late checkout to 3 p.m., subject to availability

Set expectations against Preferred Club or Platinum. Rock Royalty has no private lounge, no exclusive restaurant and no dedicated beach the way the AMR and Princess club tiers do. It buys service, spa and priority — which suits some couples and disappoints others who have stayed at a Dreams or a Princess.

Rock Suites include it, along with a personal assistant, on top of the Rock Royalty Ocean View room category itself.

  • Bowling alley at Moonlight
  • Deluxe Family suites sleep seven

The ocean is not swimmable. The resort is on the Pacific side at Diamante, not the Sea of Cortez, and warns that currents are dangerous even for experienced swimmers. Ceremonies on the sand are fine; ocean swimming, snorkelling and most water sports are not available here. Say it early — it is the most common source of disappointment on this property.

Rock Royalty is a service tier, not a club. No private lounge, restaurant or beach. Couples coming from a Dreams or a Princess will expect more than it delivers.

Every package caps ceremony seating at 32. Above that, pricing is custom — so a 60-guest wedding cannot simply be quoted from the published list.

Transfer is 45–75 minutes over roughly 51–55 km from Los Cabos International, because the resort sits past Cabo San Lucas rather than on the corridor.
